Scenario #1 
Patient Name: Mrs. Eleanor Mitchell
Age: 78 years
Background: Mrs. Eleanor Mitchell, a 78-year-old retired school teacher, has been married for 55 years to her husband, Mr. Albert Mitchell. They have one child, Laura, who lives in a different city due to her job but visits as often as she can. Eleanor has a history of multiple comorbidities, including type 2 diabetes, atrial fibrillation, hypertension, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) on 2L home O2. She also underwent a hip replacement surgery three years ago, a coronary artery bypass graft surgery five years ago, and has a history of cholecystectomy.
Current Living Situation: Before this episode, Eleanor was already somewhat frail. She relied heavily on Albert for many daily tasks and had a visiting nurse who came in twice a week to assist with her medications and monitor her overall health. Their home is a single-story house, but it's not fully equipped for someone with severe functional disabilities. They have a close-knit community and neighbors who check on them regularly. Laura, their daughter, is supportive but also has commitments in her own city and can't be present all the time.
Presenting Complaint: Eleanor was brought to the emergency department with sudden onset severe abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting. She described the pain as sharp and constant. She also noticed that her stools had become dark and tarry.
Clinical Findings: Upon examination, Eleanor's abdomen was distended with absent bowel sounds. Her blood pressure was slightly lowered, and her heart rate was elevated.
Investigations: An urgent CT angiogram revealed signs consistent with acute mesenteric ischemia, a condition where blood flow to the intestines is blocked. Lab results also showed elevated lactate levels, further confirming the diagnosis.
Clinical Decision Point: Eleanor's condition is life-threatening. The surgical team discussed the situation with her and her family. The only potential lifesaving intervention is a bowel resection surgery. However, even if successful, this surgery would leave Eleanor dependent on Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN) because a significant part of her intestines would need to be removed. This would mean she'd need intravenous nutrition for the rest of her life. Additionally, the surgery and the post-operative period would likely leave her further debilitated, making her dependent on others for most, if not all, of her activities of daily living (ADLs).
