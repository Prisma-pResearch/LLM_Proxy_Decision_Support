Scenario #4
Patient Name: Mr. Alex Turner
Age: 20 years
Background: Alex Turner is a 20-year-old university student studying literature. Known to be introverted, he struggled with severe depression and had previously sought counseling through the university’s mental health services. He lived in the university dormitory and was an only child. His parents, Mr. and Mrs. Turner, live in another city, about a three-hour drive away.
Current Living Situation: Alex lived alone in a single room in the university dormitory. He was known to have a few close friends but mostly kept to himself. His academic record was exemplary, and he was passionate about poetry and literature.
Presenting Complaint: Emergency services were called to the university dormitory after reports of a fire. Upon arrival, they found Alex with extensive burn injuries. Preliminary investigations suggest that he had attempted self-immolation in his room.
Clinical Findings: Alex was immediately taken to the nearest burn center. Upon examination, he had third-degree burns covering approximately 80% of his total body surface area (TBSA), including his face, chest, arms, and legs. Due to the severity of the burns and potential airway compromise, he was intubated and sedated.
Clinical Decision Point: The burn and surgical teams are faced with the challenge of managing extensive burn wounds, which carry a high risk of infection, complications, and mortality, especially given the large TBSA involved. The immediate priority is stabilizing Alex's condition, ensuring adequate fluid resuscitation, and preventing infection. Long-term, Alex will require multiple surgeries, skin grafts, and extensive rehabilitation. Furthermore, his prior mental health challenges and the nature of his injuries underscore the need for comprehensive psychiatric evaluation and support.
