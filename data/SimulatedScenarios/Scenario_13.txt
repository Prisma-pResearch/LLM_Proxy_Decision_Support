Scenario #11
Patient Name: Mr. Ronald Harris
Age: 76 years
Background: Mr. Ronald Harris is a 76-year-old former firefighter, celebrated for his tales of heroism and unity. Diagnosed with early-stage Alzheimer's, Ronald had been managing his symptoms reasonably well with medications. He lost his wife five years ago, and his only son, Peter, who is in his early 40s and works as a bank manager, visits him periodically.
Current Living Situation: Ronald has been transitioned to a long-term care facility, where he has a private room adorned with memorabilia from his firefighting days. The staff have grown fond of him, even with the challenges his care presents.
Presenting Complaint: Ronald was discovered by a neighbor at the foot of his staircase after a fall. Immediate medical attention revealed a significant spinal cord injury that resulted in paralysis from the waist down. Coupled with his cognitive difficulties, independent living became unfeasible.
New Developments: Over time, Ronald developed a large sacral decubitus ulcer due to his immobility and the challenges of managing his personal hygiene, given his cognitive and physical impairments. Regular soilage from bowel movements further exacerbated the wound, increasing the risk of infection and complicating wound care.
Clinical Decision Point: While treating the decubitus ulcer is a priority, the recurrent contamination from bowel movements makes wound care challenging. Peter, after discussing with the medical team and researching on his own, is leaning towards the creation of an ostomy as a solution to manage the fecal contamination, hoping it would promote wound healing and improve his father's overall quality of life. However, such a procedure, while potentially beneficial, is not without risks, especially given Ronald's age and overall health status.
