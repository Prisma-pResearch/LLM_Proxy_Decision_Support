Scenario #33
Patient Name: Mrs. Patricia (Pat) Wilson
Age: 73 years
Background: Mrs. Patricia Wilson, a retired nurse, has a history of congestive heart failure and type 2 diabetes. Over recent years, her cardiac function has notably deteriorated, leading to frequent hospital admissions. Pat is fiercely independent, often voicing her desire to "fight until the very end," and has been very involved in her medical care decisions. She has two daughters, both of whom work in non-medical professions and live out of state.
Living situation: Unknown.
Presenting Complaint: Pat was brought to the emergency department with acute respiratory distress. Investigations revealed a severe pneumonia superimposed on her already compromised cardiac function.
Clinical Situation: Despite being started on broad-spectrum antibiotics and supportive measures, Pat's condition rapidly worsened. She was shifted to the ICU, where she required mechanical ventilation. Her cardiac function continued to decline, and the medical team raised concerns about multi-organ failure.
Before her condition deteriorated, Pat had clearly communicated to her medical team her wish for maximal therapy, regardless of the prognosis. She had explicitly stated her desire for any life-sustaining interventions, including resuscitation efforts, should the need arise.
Family Input: Pat's daughters, alarmed by the sudden downturn in their mother's health, arrived at the hospital. After discussions with the medical team about the severity of Pat's condition and the potential outcomes, both felt that their mother might be undergoing undue suffering. Given her frailty and the aggressive nature of the interventions, they began to lean towards transitioning to comfort care, believing it might be a more compassionate approach.
