Scenario #8
Patient Name: Mrs. Margaret Anderson
Age: 92 years
Background: Mrs. Margaret Anderson is a 92-year-old woman who once worked as a school principal. She was diagnosed with dementia several years ago and, due to her deteriorating cognitive function, was placed in a nursing home where she receives round-the-clock care. She has outlived her spouse and friends, and her only remaining family is a niece who lives in another state.
Current Living Situation: Mrs. Anderson resides in a nursing home that caters to patients with advanced cognitive disorders. The staff are familiar with her care needs, and she has a routine that includes daily walks in the garden and music therapy. Her niece visits once a year and occasionally communicates with the nursing home staff to check on her aunt's well-being.
Presenting Complaint: The nursing home staff noticed that Mrs. Anderson has been uncomfortable and often touching her posterior region. Upon examination, they identified a rectal prolapse.
Clinical Findings: The rectal prolapse is causing Mrs. Anderson discomfort, but she is unable to communicate the extent of her symptoms effectively due to her advanced dementia. The nursing home staff have been managing it with manual reduction and supportive measures, but the prolapse frequently recurs.
Clinical Decision Point: Rectal prolapse can be surgically corrected, but given Mrs. Anderson's age, cognitive status, and overall frailty, the procedure poses significant risks. The medical team is debating whether a surgical correction, such as a rectopexy, is appropriate or whether conservative management should continue.
