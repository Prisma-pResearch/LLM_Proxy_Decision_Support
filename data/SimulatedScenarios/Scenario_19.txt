Scenario #17
Patient Name: Mrs. Eleanor Williams
Age: 80 years
Background: Mrs. Eleanor Williams, a retired elementary school principal, is known in her community for her decades of service, kindness, and dedication to children's education. A widow for the past five years, Eleanor has three children, seven grandchildren, and a few great-grandchildren. She has always been active, participating in book clubs, gardening groups, and church activities.
Current Living Situation: Eleanor lived independently in a cozy two-bedroom house filled with memories, photographs, and books. Her children live nearby and have always been involved in her life, sharing in caregiving responsibilities, especially after her husband's passing.
Presenting Complaint: Eleanor was admitted to the hospital following a severe stroke. While the stroke itself caused significant functional and cognitive impairments, complications arose when she developed a clot in her Superior Mesenteric Artery (SMA), leading to mesenteric ischemia.
Clinical Findings: Emergency surgery was performed to address the mesenteric ischemia. Unfortunately, the ischemia had affected a large portion of her small bowel, necessitating an extensive resection. Post-operatively, it became clear that Eleanor had insufficient remaining bowel to maintain adequate caloric intake. This means she would require Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN) for the rest of her life to meet her nutritional needs.
Clinical Decision Point: The medical team, in collaboration with Eleanor's children (who are her medical proxies), now faces challenging decisions regarding her long-term care. The implications of lifelong TPN, along with Eleanor's post-stroke condition, must be carefully weighed against her quality of life and previously expressed wishes about medical interventions.
