Scenario #5
Patient Name: Mrs. Grace Hamilton
Age: 70 years
Background: Mrs. Grace Hamilton is a retired librarian who has lived a rich and fulfilling life. She has two adult children and four grandchildren all of whom live in the United Kingdom. Recently, she underwent a distal bypass surgery due to peripheral vascular disease. During her post-op recovery in the hospital, she had multiple conversations with a resident doctor. She frequently expressed her contentment with the life she's lived and shared her philosophy of "letting nature run its course" rather than pursuing aggressive medical interventions.
Current Living Situation: Prior to her hospitalization for the bypass surgery, Mrs. Hamilton lived alone in a small apartment, having lost her husband five years ago. Her children visit her frequently, and she enjoys spending time reading and gardening.
Presenting Complaint: A few days post-operation, Mrs. Hamilton started showing symptoms of respiratory distress. She developed a fever, persistent cough, and difficulty breathing, quickly requiring emergent intubation and sedation. She continues to decompensate. 
Clinical Findings: Upon evaluation, Mrs. Hamilton's oxygen saturation was found to be decreasing rapidly. A chest X-ray and subsequent tests confirmed a diagnosis of severe COVID-19 pneumonia.
Clinical Decision Point: Given the severity of her pneumonia and her decreasing oxygen levels, the medical team is considering advanced interventions. Extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) could potentially support her lungs and give her a chance of recovery. However, the hospital has limited ECMO machines, and there are other critically ill COVID-19 patients who might also benefit from the intervention.
