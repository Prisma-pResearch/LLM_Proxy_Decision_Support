Scenario #36
Patient Name: Mr. William (Bill) Carter
Age: 65 years
Background: Mr. William Carter, a Vietnam war veteran, had been living alone since his wife passed away a decade ago. He was known to have chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) from a long history of smoking and a recent diagnosis of early-stage Alzheimer's disease.
Current Living Situation: Living alone at home in an apartment.
Presenting Complaint: A neighbor found Bill unresponsive in his apartment and promptly called emergency services. He was rushed to the hospital where he was intubated and placed on mechanical ventilation due to acute respiratory failure, likely secondary to a COPD exacerbation.
Clinical Situation: While Bill's respiratory status was stabilized in the ICU, further evaluation revealed a massive stroke, which was likely the precipitating event for his respiratory failure. The prognosis was grim, with minimal chance of meaningful neurological recovery.
The medical team initiated efforts to contact Bill's next of kin to discuss his care plan. According to the hospital's previous records, Bill's emergency contact was listed as his son, who lived out of state. However, multiple attempts to reach him were unsuccessful. No other immediate family members were listed or known to the hospital staff.
