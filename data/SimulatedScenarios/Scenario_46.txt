Scenario #44
Patient Name: Mr. Benjamin "Ben" Wallace
Age: 70 years
Background: Mr. Benjamin Wallace, known as "Ben" among friends and family, was a former jazz musician who played the saxophone in various bands across the country during his younger days. A few years ago, he was diagnosed with progressive multiple sclerosis (MS). This diagnosis hit him hard, as it gradually robbed him of his ability to play his beloved instrument. Yet, he transitioned into teaching music theory at a local school, refusing to be fully defeated by his ailment.
Living Situation: Living at home.
Presenting Complaint: Ben was discovered in his music room at home, slumped over his saxophone, with an empty bottle of alcohol and several medication containers nearby. It appeared he might have combined alcohol with his medications, possibly in an overdose attempt. He was immediately brought to the emergency department.
Clinical Findings: In the emergency room, it became clear that Ben had ingested a dangerous mix of alcohol with his MS medications and some painkillers. The combination could lead to severe respiratory depression. The medical team determined that a procedure to clear his stomach contents and the administration of specific antidotes could be life-saving.
Clinical Decision Point: Complicating the decision, a notebook was found beside him in the music room. In it, Ben had written a series of entries about his struggles with MS, the pain of losing his ability to play, and his wish not to undergo aggressive treatments if he ever reached a point where he could no longer recognize himself. The medical team is now faced with the ethical dilemma of honoring the sentiments Ben expressed in his personal writings while also considering the immediate life-saving measures available.
Current Living Situation: Ben lived in a vintage apartment filled with music memorabilia from his tours. He has two daughters, both of whom pursued careers in the arts, inspired by their father. They reside in different cities but visit often, cherishing the musical legacy their father passed down to them.
