Scenario #34
Patient Name: Mr. Leonard (Leo) Foster
Age: 78 years
Background: Mr. Leonard Foster, a former university professor, has been living with advanced Parkinson's disease for over a decade. His condition has been marked by progressive motor dysfunction, leading to significant mobility issues and frequent falls. He also has a history of hypertension and chronic kidney disease. Despite his health challenges, Leo has always maintained a positive outlook, actively engaging with his healthcare providers about his treatment options.
Current Living Situation: Living at home.
Presenting Complaint: Leo was admitted to the hospital following a significant fall at home, resulting in a hip fracture. During his hospital stay, he developed a hospital-acquired infection which led to sepsis.
Clinical Situation: Leo's sepsis, combined with his underlying comorbidities, resulted in a rapid decline in his health. He became hemodynamically unstable and required vasopressor support. The medical team initiated broad-spectrum antibiotics and aggressive fluid resuscitation.
During previous outpatient visits, Leo had made it clear to his physicians that he wanted all possible interventions, even if his chances of recovery were slim. He had always expressed a wish to "fight every battle, no matter the odds."
Family Input: Upon understanding the gravity of Leo's situation, his two sons, both of whom had been supportive of their father's wishes in the past, began to reconsider. Witnessing their father's deteriorating condition and the aggressive measures being taken, they started questioning the quality of life Leo might have, even if he were to recover from this acute episode. They voiced their concerns to the medical team, suggesting that perhaps it was time to focus on comfort measures rather than aggressive treatment.
