Scenario #24
Patient Name: Mr. Raymond Foster
Age: 72 years
Background: Mr. Raymond Foster, a retired marine engineer, has a known history of peripheral vascular disease (PVD), a condition he's managed for over a decade with medications and lifestyle modifications. An avid fisherman, Raymond's happiest moments were spent by the lakeside, teaching his grandchildren the intricacies of the sport. Despite his PVD, he remained active, though at a slower pace, and frequently declined more aggressive treatments for his condition, opting for conservative management.
Living Situation: Unknown
Presenting Complaint: Approximately six months ago, Raymond developed a minor wound on his left foot after a fishing trip. Given his PVD, the wound's healing was compromised. Over time, despite medical interventions, the wound worsened, becoming chronic and showing signs of deep infection. On multiple consultations, Raymond was advised to consider amputation as a definitive solution, given the extent of tissue damage and the high risk of systemic infection. He consistently and categorically refused, expressing a deep-seated fear of losing his foot and the perceived loss of autonomy that might accompany such a procedure.
Clinical Deterioration: Recently, Raymond's condition took a sharp turn for the worse. He presented to the emergency department with high fever, confusion, and hypotension. Investigations revealed that the infection from his foot had spread, leading to sepsis. With his mental state altered due to the septicemia, Raymond was unable to make or communicate decisions regarding his care.
Clinical Decision Point: Raymond's son, acting as his medical proxy, was confronted with a heart-wrenching decision. Armed with the knowledge of his father's past refusals, but also faced with the immediate threat to Raymond's life, he expressed a desire for the medical team to proceed with the amputation, hoping it would give his father a fighting chance at survival.
