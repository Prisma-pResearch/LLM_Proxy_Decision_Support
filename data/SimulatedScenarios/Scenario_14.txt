Scenario #12:
Patient Name: Mrs. Clara Martinez
Age: 72 years
Background: Mrs. Clara Martinez is a 72-year-old retired librarian, a passionate reader, and a longtime supporter of community education. Clara has a doting husband, two adult children, and several grandchildren. Over the past year, Clara's family noticed increasing forgetfulness and a decline in her ability to handle daily tasks independently. Given a strong family history of Alzheimer's disease, the family initially assumed Clara was showing early signs of dementia.
Current Living Situation: Clara and her husband, Miguel, live in a countryside home. Miguel has been Clara's primary caregiver, especially with her increasing memory issues, with their children and a home nurse offering additional support.
Presenting Complaint: Clara was recently brought to the hospital with signs of bowel obstruction, including severe abdominal pain, bloating, and vomiting. Medical evaluations revealed an obstructing mass in the colon, which was diagnosed as colon cancer. To the family's shock, subsequent investigations also revealed metastatic lesions in the brain, offering an alternative explanation to her memory and cognitive issues.
Clinical Findings: The primary colon tumor is causing significant obstructive symptoms. Clara's recent cognitive decline, earlier attributed to potential early-onset dementia, now seems related to the brain metastases. This complicates the decision-making process regarding her care.
Clinical Decision Point: The medical team is contemplating palliative debulking surgery for Clara's colon tumor to alleviate the obstructive symptoms. The surgery isn't curative but aims to improve quality of life. However, the presence of brain metastases and their potential influence on her cognitive function adds complexity to the decision.
