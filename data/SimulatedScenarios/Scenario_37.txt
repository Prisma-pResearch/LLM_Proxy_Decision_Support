Scenario #35
Patient Name: Ms. Alexandra (Alex) Hayes
Age: 52 years
Background: Ms. Alexandra Hayes, a former journalist, was diagnosed with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) seven years ago. ALS, a progressive neurodegenerative disease, has severely affected her muscle function, rendering her almost completely paralyzed. She communicates primarily through an eye-tracking device. Prior to her diagnosis, Alex was an avid traveler, often writing about her adventures in various international publications.
Current Living Situation: Unknown
Presenting Complaint: Alex was admitted to the hospital for a routine respiratory assessment, given the progression of her ALS and its impact on her respiratory muscles. During her admission, she experienced a significant respiratory compromise requiring BiPAP support.
Clinical Situation: After stabilization, Alex, through her communication device, expressed a clear and consistent desire not to continue with life-sustaining measures, including BiPAP. She conveyed feelings of profound suffering due to her condition, stating that the progression of her ALS had robbed her of a life she recognized and valued. Alex mentioned that she had contemplated end-of-life options in jurisdictions where assisted dying is legal and had been researching this prior to her recent hospitalization.
Family Input: Alex's siblings, who had been her primary caregivers since the ALS diagnosis, were torn. While they deeply empathized with her suffering, the thought of actively assisting or supporting her wish to end her life was emotionally challenging. They struggled with understanding her perspective, grappling with their feelings of loss, guilt, and the ethical implications of her request.
