Scenario #3:
Patient Name: Mr. Robert "Rob" Dawson
Age: 58 years
Background: Mr. Robert Dawson is a 58-year-old homeless veteran. He served two tours overseas during his younger years but struggled with post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) upon his return. This led to difficulties in maintaining stable employment and relationships. Over the years, he's become estranged from any family and has been living on the streets, occasionally accessing veteran support services and shelters.
Current Living Situation: Rob mostly lives on the streets, occasionally accessing shelters during extreme weather conditions. He has a few close friends among the homeless community who look out for one another but has no reliable or consistent support system. He often relies on soup kitchens and veteran outreach programs for meals.
Presenting Complaint: Over the past year, Rob has been losing weight and noticed blood in his stool. Recently, he started experiencing abdominal pain and increased fatigue.
Clinical Findings: On examination, Rob looked malnourished with signs of peripheral vascular disease evident in his lower extremities. A digital rectal exam revealed a palpable mass.
Investigations: Work up revealed a T4 rectal tumor. A subsequent CT scan showed the tumor had invaded nearby structures but no distant metastasis.
Clinical Decision Point Before Incident: The oncology team discussed Rob's condition with him. Given the size and location of the tumor, combined with his peripheral vascular disease, chemoradiation would typically be the first line of treatment. However, considering Rob's unstable living situation, there are concerns about his ability to reliably attend appointments and manage potential side effects. The alternative would be a radical surgery, a pelvic extirpation, which would result in both a urostomy and colostomy. After understanding the implications, Rob stated he did not want the surgery, citing his inability to manage the post-operative requirements.
Acute Development: Shortly after, Rob was brought into the emergency department with signs of severe sepsis. Investigations revealed a perforation in the rectum, likely from the tumor, leading to the sepsis. Rob's condition deteriorated rapidly, and he became unconscious, unable to provide consent or make decisions for himself.
